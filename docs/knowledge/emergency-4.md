# Emergency 4 (911: First Responders)

## What the game is **(source)**

* Real-time strategy from the player as incident commander: police, fire brigade, emergency medical service and technical services ("Tech") are commanded together.
* Controls: left mouse button selects, right mouse button gives the context order (move, or act on whatever is under the cursor). Isometric camera in the older games, free 3D camera from Emergency 3 on.
* Emergency 4 has 25+ vehicles plus on-foot rescue personnel, a campaign of 20 missions and two free-play styles (Endless: a continuous stream of everyday emergencies, Challenge: a high-score mode). It was the first with online co-op.
* Missions mix scripted big events (plane crash, flood, train accident, bomb threat) with background "dynamic" emergencies: car accidents, medical emergencies, crimes, fires, missing persons, pipe ruptures.
* Campaign missions open with a briefing and require choosing which units to deploy; the Tech forces got stronger abilities in 4 (wheel loaders for clearing, recovery helicopter with winch).
* Fire can spread, so an unattended fire becomes more incidents.

## Mechanics worth copying **(general knowledge)**

1. **Vehicles are tools that carry people.** You send the vehicle, then dismount the crew, and the crew do the work on foot. Equipment decides what a crew can do (hose, rescue tools, stretcher, handcuffs).
2. **Right-click is context sensitive.** A fire engine ordered onto a burning house starts extinguishing; a paramedic ordered onto a casualty treats them.
3. **Patients go through a chain:** treat on scene, load, drive to hospital. The ambulance is a bottleneck.
4. **Police arrest, secure, disperse.** Crowd control and area closure are objectives, not decoration.
5. **Tech clears the road.** Obstacles block routes until cleared; the right vehicle (crane, tow truck) matters.
6. **Failure is gradual.** Casualties lose health with time, fires grow, calls expire: the player triages under time pressure.
7. **Hydrants and water supply** limit sustained fire fighting; water tankers extend it.
