# 911 Operator and 112 Operator

Both are dispatcher simulations: the player sits in the control room, not on the street. They complement the Emergency series, which starts where the dispatcher stops.

## What they do **(source)**

* **Call taking.** Calls arrive with a time limit; you question the caller, classify the incident and decide on the response. Sometimes first-aid instructions over the phone are enough, sometimes police, fire or paramedics are needed, sometimes the call is a prank or can be ignored. 911 Operator lists 140+ report types; callers can be pranksters or need CPR instructions (112 Operator).
* **Triage under scarcity.** Overlapping incidents and limited vehicles force you to deprioritise low-risk calls. Incidents are colour coded by service (police, rescue, fire); multi-colour incidents need several services.
* **Unit management.** Specialist teams (SWAT, helicopters, riot units, dogs, robots, doctors, sergeants) can be added. 112 Operator also has a tactical view and an "OnSite" view of the scene.
* **Context.** Weather, day/night, seasons and traffic change difficulty; behaviour differs per district type (slums, business, residential, industrial, forest) and per country's legislation.
* **Consequences.** Outcomes show up in an after-duty report and in the department's earnings; in career mode mistakes lead to reprimands.
* **Modes.** Career (cities with unique events), scenarios, free play with your own rules.

## Open ideas

* A proper **call queue**: calls ring, you pick up, ask 2-3 questions (what, where, how many, is anybody hurt), and only then dispatch. Information quality drives which units the control room recommends.
* **After-duty report** with response times, wasted dispatches, casualties by outcome.
* **Districts** with different incident mixes (industrial: chemical/fire, residential: medical/domestic, centre: crowds/crime).
* **Hospital capacity**: patients wait if the nearest hospital is full.
* **Career**: ranks and unlocks, difficulty by city.
