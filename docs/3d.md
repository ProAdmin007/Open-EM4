# 3D view

The game has two views that read the same `World` and share the same HUD, audio and commands: the original 2D Phaser
view (`src/game/scene.ts`) and an experimental 3D view (`src/game/three-renderer.ts`, three.js). Switch between them
in **Instellingen → Weergave** or the quick toggle button on the main menu; the choice is remembered in
`localStorage` (`storage.ts`, `Settings.viewMode`).

## What works

- **Scenery**: a single baked-canvas ground texture (roads with lane markings and curbs, grass, parking bays, water),
  procedural buildings (box + gable/hip/flat roof, coloured per building type, from `floors`/`roof` in the map data,
  with the gable ridge always rotated to follow the footprint's longer side), instanced trees.
- **Entities**: vehicles, on-foot people (responders, victims, suspects, bystanders, pedestrians) and civilian cars are
  built from `createSnapshot()` every frame and diffed against a `Map<id, Object3D>` (create/update/remove), the same
  pattern `GameScene.track` already used for 2D sprites. Vehicles are coloured by service with a blinking light bar;
  responders get a role-coloured body.
- **Fire**: a fixed-size particle pool (flame + smoke) spawns from burning buildings, scaled by fire intensity.
- **Incidents**: a floating emoji billboard above the incident location (same icon as the 2D view).
- **Traffic lights**: a bulb per intersection corner, coloured from `World.lightPhase`.
- **Camera**: an RTS-style rig — perspective camera orbiting a ground target. WASD pans, the scroll wheel zooms,
  holding the middle mouse button rotates (not Q/E, since `E` is already the dismount shortcut — see below).
- **Input**: left-click selects, drag box-selects (with a translucent ground highlight), right-click issues the usual
  move/context order, double-right-click on the selected vehicle dismounts everyone. All of this goes through
  `src/game/selection.ts` (`Commands`, `pickVehicle`, `pickUnit`), extracted from `GameScene` so the 2D and 3D input
  share one implementation instead of two. Keyboard shortcuts (E/R/X/H/Z/B/1/2/3/Space/Esc) are unchanged — they are
  still Phaser keys on `GameScene`, which keeps running underneath (ticking the world, HUD, audio) whichever view is
  on screen.
- **Lazy loading**: `GameScene.setViewMode('3d')` does a dynamic `import('./three-renderer')`, so three.js (≈520 KB
  gzipped ≈130 KB) is a separate chunk that only loads if a player actually switches to 3D; the default 2D build is
  unaffected.
- **Tests**: `tests/unit/three-renderer.test.ts` covers the ray-picking math (`pickGroundTile`/`projectTileToNDC`,
  pure three.js math, no WebGL/canvas needed) and the shared selection logic (`Commands`, box-select, double-right-click
  dismount) against a real `World`. `tests/e2e/gameplay.spec.ts` has a full scenario test in the 3D view: select the
  engine, right-click the burning building (ray-picked through the 3D camera), dismount, extinguish, the incident
  disappears.

## World dressing (`three-world.ts`)

Inspired by the look of Emergency 4 city maps and the Los Angeles mod (palm trees, signs, barriers and cones); no assets are copied.

- **Ground** (`three-ground.ts`): baked in 16x16-tile chunks at 40 px per tile (about 9 px per metre) with seamless fractal noise for asphalt, pavement, grass and water, plus crisp vector detail — dashed centre lines, edge lines, zebra crossings next to every junction, worn wheel tracks, gutters, manholes, patches and cracks, 1 m pavement slabs with joints, bay lines at the stations, ponds with a sandy shore. The pavements are real geometry, raised 14 cm above the road with kerb walls. A grass plane extends beyond the map edge.
- **Facades and roofs** (`three-textures.ts`): shared procedural detail maps multiplied with the building colour — brick courses on houses, schools and stations, concrete panels on flats and shops, glass curtain walls on offices and hospitals, corrugated cladding on factories and warehouses, stone on churches, roof tiles on pitched roofs and gravel on flat ones.
- **Street furniture** (instanced / merged): street lamps (glow at night), fire hydrants, benches, litter bins and bus shelters along the pavements.
- **Vegetation**: broadleaf trees, conifers, palm trees and bushes with per-instance colour variation.
- **Buildings**: windows as two instanced meshes (lit / dark), plus per-type details — porches, chimneys, hedges and garages on houses; balconies and rooftop units on flats; awnings and signs on shops; glass-band offices with an aircraft-warning light; chimneys on factories; loading doors on warehouses; a helipad, canopy and red cross at hospitals; a flagpole at schools; a bell tower at churches; garage doors, a sign and sometimes a hose tower at stations. Collapsed buildings lose their windows and details.
- **Environment**: a sky gradient dome, a sun that moves through a 24-minute game day (about 08:30 at the start; dusk, night with lit windows and lamps, dawn) and soft shadows from the sun that follow the camera. Both can be switched off in *Instellingen* (`openrescue.shadows` / `openrescue.daynight` in `localStorage`); automated tests (`?e2e`) run in constant daylight without shadows, `?e2e&fx` turns the effects back on for screenshots.

## Fire and incidents

- **Fire**: three soft-particle pools (additive flames, dark billowing smoke, water) in `three-fx.ts` — flames through the windows and out of the roof, embers, smoke that grows and drifts, flickering point lights on the three strongest fires, buildings that char with damage and collapse to a low ruin when destroyed.
- **Firefighting**: hoses are drawn from the engine to every crew member working with water, and a ballistic water jet is sprayed at the burning building once the hose is deployed.
- **Incidents**: a pulsing ground ring and light beam per incident, wrecked cars and debris at accidents, barriers/fallen trees/wrecks on obstacle tiles, police tape around secured scenes.
- **Vehicles** (`three-models.ts`): fire engine, aerial ladder, rescue and command vehicles, ambulance, rapid responder, MMT, patrol car, van, motorcycle, tow truck, service van and crane truck each have their own livery, plus sedans, hatchbacks and vans for civilian traffic.

- **Operations**: the aerial ladder raises, turns towards the burning building and extends while its crew sprays; vehicles hooked up to a hydrant get a supply hose from the hydrant; the bomb squad's robot rolls out to the suspicious package; secured scenes get tape, cones, barriers and glowing road flares.

- **Fire look**: flames are flame-shaped billboards (a teardrop shader with a wobble and a white-hot core) that lean out of the windows, a soft heat glow over the roof, reddish-black smoke, sparks, orange flickering windows that spread across the facade as the fire grows, and flickering point lights.
- **Medical and accident scenes**: paramedics kneel and do chest compressions, a medical bag stands beside them, treated casualties lie on a trolley under a blanket, pale skin at low health, blood pools at trauma incidents, emergency foil on the deceased, ambulance rear doors that swing open while a patient is loaded. Road accidents have tyre marks, an oil puddle, scattered glass, steaming wrecks and a warning triangle; burglaries get a flashing alarm beacon.

- **Explosion and cordon**: a failed bomb call plays a flash, fireball, shockwave ring, debris, a dark smoke plume, a point light and a short camera shake; secured scenes put barriers on the road at the edge of the closed area (`CORDON_RADIUS` in the simulation).

## Known gaps

- **Models are still procedural** (boxes and cylinders, no animation rig): people have limbs that swing while walking and raise while working, vehicles have per-type bodies, wheels that spin, alternating light bars and head/tail lights, but no ladder or crane animation.
- **No shadows** (perf/time trade-off for the first version); lighting is a hemisphere + one directional light.
- **Spatial audio** still uses the 2D camera's centre (`GameScene.update` reads `renderer3d.cameraTile()` for this,
  so panning does track the 3D camera target, but distance falloff is tuned for the 2D top-down view).
- **Performance** (measured honestly, not from the stale `game.loop.actualFps`, via `tools/bench3d.mjs` against the
  production build under Playwright's software-rendered SwiftShader Chrome — a deliberately pessimistic baseline):
  2D ≈ 12 fps, 3D ≈ 10 fps on "hard" freeplay (≈20 vehicles, hundreds of pedestrians/cars). The 3D scene itself is
  light (≈16k triangles, ~36 draw calls at a typical zoom — mostly Lambert-shaded boxes), so real GPU hardware should
  comfortably clear 60 fps; software rendering is not representative of a real browser.
- **No multi-file asset pipeline**: everything is procedural (boxes, cones, capsules, a baked canvas texture), per
  the "no external assets" rule — see `THIRD_PARTY.md`. A future version could map `model` ids to real glTF models
  without touching the simulation or the snapshot API.

## Coordinate conventions (unchanged, already in `src/sim/space.ts`)

- Simulation space: tile units, +x east, +y south, a tile is 1×1. Entity positions are tile centres (x.5, y.5).
- Heading: radians, `atan2(dy, dx)` in simulation space (0 = east, π/2 = south).
- 3D world space (three.js, right-handed, Y up): X = east, Z = south, Y = up, in metres. `TILE_METERS = 4.5`.
- `tileToWorld3D` / `world3DToTile` convert between the two; `headingToYaw` converts a heading to a Y-axis rotation
  for a model whose front points along +X.
